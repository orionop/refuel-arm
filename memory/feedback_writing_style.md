Conversational but technical. Writes like he's explaining to a smart peer, not performing for a committee.

**Tone markers:**
- Contractions and informal openers are fine: "isn't", "it's", "that too", "rather"
- Rhetorical framing: states the problem plainly before introducing the solution ("There exists no such cohesive pipeline...")
- Uses quotes around informal terms: "noisy" rollouts, "ghosts", "exploits", "refuel"
- Avoids passive voice where possible — active, direct sentences preferred
- Numbers and precision stated plainly: "8.2cm", "$\sim 10^{-16}$ m", "up to 8 roots"

**Sentence rhythm:**
- Short punchy sentences after a longer setup: "This level of demand for precision rules out any discussion about surface level solutions entirely."
- Transitions are explicit but not stiff: "The layer above involves...", "Finally, a reactive obstacle avoidance system kicks in."
- Does not over-hedge — states results and decisions confidently

**What to avoid:**
- Formal academic throat-clearing ("It is worth noting that...", "As can be seen from...")
- Em dashes (already flagged — banned)
- AI-sounding filler: "robust", "seamlessly", "leveraging", "state-of-the-art"
- Overly long sentences with multiple nested clauses

**Why:** Abstract and Chapter 1 are the gold standard sections — off-limits for edits, used as style reference for all other chapters.
